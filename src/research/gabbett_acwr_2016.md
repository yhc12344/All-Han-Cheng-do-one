# Gabbett's Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR)

**Author:** Dr. Tim J. Gabbett  
**Year:** 2016  
**Journal:** British Journal of Sports Medicine (BJSM)  
**Core Concept:** The Training-Injury Prevention Paradox  

---

## 📋 Theoretical Overview

The Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR) compares the training load accumulated over a short-term window (the "acute" load, typically representing fatigue) with the training load accumulated over a longer-term window (the "chronic" load, typically representing fitness). 

In sports science, it is widely recognized as the single most validated clinical and empirical model for predicting musculoskeletal tissue failure (injury risk) under athletic stress.

---

## 🧮 Mathematical Formulation

The ACWR can be calculated using either a simple rolling average (RA) or an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA). In the context of exponentially smoothed models (like standard Banister or Coggan CTL/ATL models):

$$\text{Acute Workload (7 Days)} = \text{ATL (Fatigue)}$$
$$\text{Chronic Workload (28/42 Days)} = \text{CTL (Fitness)}$$

The ratio is computed as:

$$\text{ACWR} = \frac{\text{Acute Workload}}{\text{Chronic Workload}}$$

---

## 🚦 Risk Zones & Pacing Sweet Spot

Dr. Gabbett's research identified distinct safety and danger boundaries for the ACWR:

*   **ACWR < 0.8: Undertraining Zone**  
    *   *Effect:* High risk of relative fitness drop (detraining).
    *   *Action:* Fitness is decaying; load can be safely increased.
*   **0.8 ≤ ACWR ≤ 1.3: "The Sweet Spot"**  
    *   *Effect:* Optimal training adaptation and fitness progression.
    *   *Injury Risk:* Lowest ($\le 5\%$).
    *   *Action:* Maintain current steady progression rate.
*   **1.3 < ACWR < 1.5: Caution Zone**  
    *   *Effect:* Accelerated load spike.
    *   *Injury Risk:* Moderately elevated.
    *   *Action:* Hold current load steady; avoid further immediate volume jumps.
*   **ACWR ≥ 1.5: "The Danger Zone"**  
    *   *Effect:* Massive training spike (relative overreaching).
    *   *Injury Risk:* Exponentially spikes up to $15-20\%$.
    *   *Action:* Immediately taper volume, prioritize active Zone 2 recovery, and schedule rest days.

---

## 🏃 Relevance to Recreational Running

The ACWR is particularly crucial for recreational marathon runners (like HC) who exhibit a pattern of **clustered training** (weeks of low activity followed by sudden high-intensity clusters). 

While absolute fatigue (TSB) might not appear critical at low absolute fitness levels, the relative acute load spike (ACWR) captures the musculoskeletal strain of rapid volume jumps, serving as an early-warning injury prevention guard.
