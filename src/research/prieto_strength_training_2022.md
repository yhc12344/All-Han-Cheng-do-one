# Running-Specific Strength Training & Running Economy

**Authors:** P. Prieto-González, J. Sedlacek  
**Journal:** International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health  
**Year:** 2022  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph191710773

## 🔬 Core Theory: Strength & Economy Concurrent Training
This 12-week study evaluated concurrent training protocols on recreational endurance runners. It proved that integrating **running-specific heavy strength and explosive plyometric work** significantly boosts physical endurance without triggering aerobic interference.

### 🏃 1. Running Economy (RE) Explained
Running Economy is defined as the steady-state volume of oxygen ($V_{\text{O}_2}$) consumed at a given submaximal running speed:
*   Improving RE means you consume **less oxygen and burn fewer glycogen stores** to maintain your goal pace (e.g., target sub-3:30 marathon pace).
*   It acts as a metabolic "efficiency tuner"—allowing you to run faster and longer with identical cardiorespiratory strain.

### 🏋️ 2. The 3-Day Strength Protocol
To maximize maximum lower-body strength, neural drive, and tendon stiffness, runners should integrate structured strength workouts **3 days a week on non-consecutive days** (e.g., Mondays, Wednesdays, Fridays):
1.  **Running-Specific Heavy Resistance Work:** Barbell squats, Bulgarian split squats, walking lunges, and calf step-ups (targeting 3-4 sets of 6-8 repetitions at 80% 1RM). Focus on clean, high-velocity concentric lifts.
2.  **Explosive Plyometrics:** Box jumps, drop jumps, single-leg bounding, and split-squat jumps. This improves stretch-shortening cycle (SSC) muscle stiffness, storing and releasing free elastic energy with each foot strike.
3.  **Dynamic Core Stability:** Planks, hanging leg raises, and rotational movements to prevent pelvic drop and mechanical power leakage during late-marathon fatigue.

### 🗓️ 3. Integration Guidelines
*   **Rest Periods:** Keep strength sessions on endurance "rest" days or separated from hard interval runs (Tuesdays/Sundays) by at least **6 to 8 hours** to avoid musculoskeletal overload and facilitate concurrent glycogen recovery.
