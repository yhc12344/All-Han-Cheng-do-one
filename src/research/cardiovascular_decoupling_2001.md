# Cardiovascular Drift and Durability in Prolonged Exercise

**Author:** Edward F. Coyle, J. González-Alonso (2001); Ed Maunder, Stephen Seiler (2021)  
**Journal:** Exercise and Sport Sciences Reviews / Sports Medicine  
**Year:** 2001 / 2021  

---

## 🔬 Core Theory
During steady-state endurance exercise, an athlete's heart rate progressively increases even if the mechanical workload (pace/power) remains perfectly flat. This is known as **Cardiovascular Drift**.

### Why it happens:
*   Core temperature rises, redirecting blood flow to the skin for cooling.
*   Sweat loss decreases blood plasma volume, reducing venous return.
*   To maintain a constant **Cardiac Output** ($\text{Cardiac Output} = \text{Stroke Volume} \times \text{Heart Rate}$), the heart rate must drift upwards as stroke volume falls.

## 📊 Aerobic Decoupling Metric
Coaches quantify this drift by dividing a workout into two equal halves and comparing the **Efficiency Factor** ($\text{EF} = \text{Pace or Power} / \text{Heart Rate}$):

$$\text{Decoupling} \% = \left( \frac{\text{EF}_{\text{first half}} - \text{EF}_{\text{second half}}}{\text{EF}_{\text{first half}}} \right) \times 100$$

*   **Decoupling < 5%:** Denotes high aerobic endurance and physiological durability. The athlete's cardiovascular system is stable and adapted.
*   **Decoupling > 5%:** Denotes cardiac fatigue, dehydration, or an underdeveloped aerobic foundation.
*   **Training Application:** If an athlete has a decoupling above 5% on base runs, they should restrict high-intensity threshold work and focus on building low-heart-rate base miles.

## 🧠 Autonomic Reactivation (HRR)
After maximal effort, the speed at which the heart rate returns to normal represents parasympathetic reactivation (vagal tone). A drop of **$\ge 40$ beats in 1 minute** represents exceptional autonomic health, whereas a drop of **$< 12$ beats** signals extreme systemic fatigue and high autonomic strain, requiring extended rest.
