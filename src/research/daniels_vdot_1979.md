**Author:** Jack Daniels, Jimmy Gilbert  
**Publisher:** Oxendine Publishing  
**Year:** 1979

## 🔬 Core Theory
Laboratory-measured VO2 Max does not always predict running success because it ignores **Running Economy** (the oxygen cost to run at a specific speed). 

Daniels created **VDOT** (a "pseudo-VO2 Max" or "Velocity VO2 Max") which mathematically combines cardiovascular capacity with running economy. VDOT is computed by dividing the oxygen cost of a given running speed ($V_{\text{O}_2}$) by the percentage of VDOT that can be sustained for that duration ($\%_{\text{Max}}$):

### 1. Oxygen Cost ($V_{\text{O}_2}$) at Speed $v$ (m/min)
$$V_{\text{O}_2} = -4.60 + 0.182258 \cdot v + 0.000104 \cdot v^2$$

### 2. Sustained Capacity Percentage ($\%_{\text{Max}}$) for Time $t$ (min)
$$\%_{\text{Max}} = 0.8 + 0.1894393 \cdot e^{-0.012778 \cdot t} + 0.2989558 \cdot e^{-0.1932605 \cdot t}$$

### 3. VDOT Equation
$$\text{VDOT} = \frac{V_{\text{O}_2}}{\%_{\text{Max}}}$$

## 🏃 Prescribing Training Intensity Zones
Once an athlete's peak VDOT is derived from race personal records, training zones are strictly prescribed:
*   **Easy Paces (E - 59-74% VDOT):** Builds mitochondria, capillary beds, and cardiac muscle. Should form 80% of weekly volume.
*   **Marathon Pace (M - 75-84% VDOT):** Aerobic capacity simulation, crucial for building endurance durability.
*   **Threshold Pace (T - 85-88% VDOT / Lactate Threshold):** Crucial for raising the speed at which blood lactate levels begin to accumulate rapidly. Must be run at a steady, "comfortably hard" effort.
*   **Interval Pace (I - 95-100% VDOT / VO2 Max):** Maximizes cardiovascular stroke volume. Sessions should limit active intervals to 5 minutes to prevent premature neuromuscular collapse.
