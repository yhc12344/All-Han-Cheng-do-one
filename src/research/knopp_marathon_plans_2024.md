# Quantitative Analysis of 92 12-Week Sub-elite Marathon Training Plans

**Authors:** M. Knopp, D. Appelhans, M. Schönfelder, S. Seiler, H. Wackerhage  
**Journal:** Sports Medicine - Open  
**Year:** 2024  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-024-00717-5  

---

## 🔬 Core Theory & Quantitative Review
This meta-analytical review analyzed **92 different 12-week marathon training programs** targeted at sub-elite and competitive recreational runners to establish optimal distributions of training load, weekly volume progression, long run caps, and taper structures.

### 📈 1. Weekly Volume & Progression Curves
*   **Volume Peak:** Average weekly volume progresses linearly, peaking at **Week 8 or 9** before initiating a 3-week linear taper.
*   **Intensity Distribution:** The vast majority of plans adhere to a polarized or pyramidal intensity distribution, where approximately **80% of weekly runs are executed in Zone 1-2 (conversational easy base)**, and 15-20% at or above threshold.

### 🏃 2. The Sunday Long Run Envelope
*   **Peak Duration:** The classic long run reaches a peak of **28 to 32 km (17 to 20 miles)**.
*   **The 3-Hour Capping Rule:** Sports-science data indicates that running continuously beyond **180 minutes (3 hours)** causes a disproportionate spike in neuromuscular micro-damage, stress hormones (cortisol), and injury risk, with diminishing returns on mitochondrial and capillary adaptations. Long runs should be capped at 3 hours regardless of target distance.

### 📉 3. The 3-Week Taper Protocol
To maximize glycogen stores, muscle repair, and TSB (Form) freshness for race day, plans utilize a structured, step-down taper:
*   **10 Days to 3 Weeks Out (Week 10):** Reduce total volume by **20% to 25%** of peak mileage.
*   **Week 11:** Reduce volume by **40% to 50%**.
*   **Race Week (Week 12):** Reduce volume by **60% to 70%** (excluding the marathon itself).
*   *Note:* Throughout the taper, running frequency and high-intensity interval intervals should be maintained to prevent aerobic detraining.
